1 Region / data residency is a compliance gate, not a convenience

Lived · MovieMotions
The only reranker that runs on my free plan (Amazon Rerank 1.0, first-party) isn't offered in us-east-1 where my stack lives — it's in us-west-2. A rerank call is stateless, so I route only that one call cross-region. For public TMDB data the only cost is latency.
Healthcare translation
For PHI, that hop becomes a gate cleared before any code. Three questions: is the service HIPAA-eligible and in your AWS BAA for that region; does the data leave the permitted boundary (us. vs global. inference profiles, now with teeth); is the vendor a Business Associate. Classification drives region/model/vendor — before performance.
"In a regulated domain, which region and which model aren't performance decisions — they're compliance gates you clear before any code, because the data's classification decides them."

2 First-party vs third-party model = who is allowed to see the data

Lived · MovieMotions
Cohere's reranker is a paid AWS Marketplace product; Amazon's is first-party. On a free plan that difference surfaced as a billing wall — the plan couldn't complete the Marketplace subscription.
Healthcare translation
The same line is a compliance firewall. Sending PHI to a third-party model makes that vendor a subprocessor touching PHI, which legally requires a Business Associate Agreement — or it's a breach. "Which vendor" is a legal question before it's a quality or cost one.
"Picking between a first-party and a third-party model in healthcare isn't about quality or price first — it's whether that vendor is contractually allowed to see the data at all."

3 Least privilege + separation of duties

Lived · MovieMotions
The app identity had bedrock:InvokeModel only; enabling reranking was a deliberate new grant (bedrock:Rerank). The app has no power to subscribe / purchase (aws-marketplace:Subscribe) — that's an account-owner action.
Healthcare translation
An app identity touching member data holds the minimum actions for its job and nothing that can spend money or change access. Separation of duties — the service invokes, a human/owner purchases and grants — is auditable, and exactly what a security review checks.
"Least privilege by kind, not just amount — the service can call the model, but it can't buy one or grant itself access. Separate identities, on purpose."

4 Semantic layer — one undefined term = quietly inconsistent answers

Already a healthcare example — the sandbox data was synthetic member enrollment.
Lived · MovieMotions
Over an 81-row enrollment file, "how many active members?" returned 75 / 72 / 69 / 62 / 60 depending on who defined "active" — and a text-to-SQL agent silently picked one, in bold, no caveat. Fixed with a governed view whose definition + reasoning live in its SQL COMMENT.
Healthcare translation
This is the healthcare problem. Enrollment, eligibility, active membership drive payments and regulatory filings. An LLM re-deriving "active member" from column names each query produces confident numbers that disagree. The fix is a semantic layer nobody can route around — and the hard part is coverage, not the first definition.
"A natural-language interface over member data is only as trustworthy as the definitions beneath it. 'Active member' gets defined once, governed, and made impossible to bypass — or you ship confident numbers that quietly disagree."

5 Graceful degradation — availability vs quality

Lived · MovieMotions
When the reranker became unreachable, search() catches the failure, logs one line, and falls back to vector order. The app keeps answering.
Healthcare translation
A member-facing system should degrade in quality under a vendor outage, not go down. Quality is best-effort; availability is protected. That SLA posture is what ops and reviewers care about — a deliberate design choice, not an accident.
"External model calls are wrapped so a vendor outage costs answer quality, never system availability."

6 Metrics can hide regressions in regulated AI

Lived · MovieMotions
An eval passed "4 of 5" before and after a change — but one query silently went from 3 correct answers to 1, because the metric only asked "did any right answer appear?" I changed it to report 3/3 vs 1/3 so decay is visible.
Healthcare translation
In a governed AI system, a coarse quality metric is a compliance risk — it lets a model degrade while the dashboard stays green. Model governance means metrics sensitive enough to see drift, a fixed golden set, and a recorded baseline you can defend in an audit.
"A pass rate without a definition of 'pass' hides regressions. In a regulated system the metric has to be sensitive enough to catch decay, or 'still passing' means nothing."

7 A better model exposes thin content — it doesn't fix it

Lived · MovieMotions
I turned on the reranker — a "smarter" stage that reads query + film together. It helped the nuanced queries (case 1: 1→2 correct; case 4: 1→2) but dropped Finding Nemo for "father and son separated" — its indexed text only says "family bond," "parental love," never the literal words, and a cross-encoder reads more literally than a blurry vector. The better model didn't add the signal; it exposed the content never had it. Coarse pass-count said 4→3; honest recall said 5/7 > 4/7.
Healthcare translation
You can't out-model thin source data. A stronger LLM over sparsely-documented member records surfaces the gaps, not fills them — and a coarse metric misreads that as "the model got worse." Governance = measuring at a grain that sees it, and investing in the content the model reads (documentation, enrichment), not just a bigger model.
"A better model doesn't invent information that isn't in the data — it exposes where the data is thin. The fix is better documentation, not a bigger model."

8 A model gateway: fast for public data, a liability for PHI

Lived · MovieMotions
Every in-house AWS reranker path was walled — a Marketplace subscription the free plan couldn't complete, a per-region grant the account lacked. OpenRouter, a provider-agnostic gateway, routed around all of it: one endpoint, one call, a vendor picked underneath. For public TMDB data, pure upside.
Healthcare translation
The convenience — one endpoint fronting many vendors — is the liability for PHI. The gateway and the model it routes to both become subprocessors touching PHI, each needing a BAA, and you've lost visibility into where the data went. The mature call inverts the hobby one: keep the model first-party / in-VPC, even at a cost, so every party that sees the data is named and contracted.
"A model gateway is right for public data and wrong for PHI — its whole convenience is not knowing which vendor served you, which is exactly what a BAA regime forbids."
